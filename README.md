# Mega Rockets — 0.8.1 Alpha
Minecraft 1.21.1 Fabric, Java 21. Titan-1 is 64 blocks tall.

## Moon
Nominal radius 4096, diameter 8192. Minecraft 1.21.1 permits only 4064 blocks of vertical build height. The spherical terrain is split into three 3072-block-high dimensions with global Y = local Y + offset:
- megarockets:moon — northern region, offset +3072; landing pad local Y=1280.
- megarockets:moon_middle — equatorial region, offset 0.
- megarockets:moon_south — southern region, offset -3072.
All regions share the same sphere and deterministic terrain, but transitions between the regions are not seamless. Use operator commands to explore other regions. Crust is nominally 192 blocks thick with caves, ores, mountains and craters; the inner core is hollow. Gravity is 16.5% normal and points down, not toward the center. Chunk generation is on demand. Test in a new world; old terrain/height settings are incompatible.

Five original 32×32 pixel textures for lunar blocks; separate metal hull texture, 72 radial segments, thermal tiles, panels, windows, navigation lights, engine rings, landing feet and longer exhaust. Max ascent and lunar transfer speed 300 blocks/s at 20 TPS; landing speed stays low. Space path remains 6000 blocks and actual world coordinates; visual planets are separate sky geometry. The Moon is not physically in the orbit dimension; transfer before descent.

## 0.6.1 Visual correction
Earth and Moon sky disks use real satellite map data, projected and shaded into 1024px PNGs at build time. Earth: NASA GSFC / Reto Stöckli, Robert Simmon and MODIS teams, Blue Marble 2002 (Wikimedia Commons archived 2048px image, public domain). Moon: NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit, Ernie Wright / LRO LROC. See ASSET_CREDITS.md. The Moon has no artificial atmosphere; the Earth limb is narrower, with an altitude-dependent apparent size. Exhaust is white/yellow/orange in all environments; vacuum changes its spread, not its palette. Existing worlds can be reused; terrain is unchanged. The disk images are fixed viewpoints, not live 3D terrain. Building requires internet access to the texture sources.

## 0.6.2 Lunar relief
Landing site is now a highland at local Y=1280, with a small 24-block-radius pad and surrounding terrain below it within 192 blocks. More irregular multiscale ridges, terraces, rock steps and debris. The old landing site's generated chunks are incompatible; use a NEW world (or a backed-up world with ungenerated lunar terrain). Autopilot refuses to land on old or obstructed landing blocks instead of overwriting them. Planet radius and flight speed unchanged.

## 0.7.0 Living Moon
Four registered lunar biomes (plains, highlands, crater fields, luminous forests) with multiscale seed-driven terrain and gradual relief transitions. Seed derived deterministically from the world-generation noise seed, shared across all three slices. New worlds with different seeds get different mountains, craters, caves, ores and trees; identical seeds reproduce terrain. Lunar trees have original bark/end-grain/leaf textures, custom logs and gently glowing leaves. Trees generate in forests and occasionally plains, never near the landing pad. Tree placement is deterministic across chunk borders. These trees are decorative world generation; saplings, recipes and regrowth are not implemented. Use a new world for the new biome source and terrain. The finite sphere, hollow core and existing landing site remain. This is a custom generator inspired by vanilla, not the vanilla Overworld generator.

## 0.7.1 startup hotfix
Register lunar wood using PillarBlock (axis) and foliage using LeavesBlock (distance, persistence, waterlogging), matching the copied vanilla settings. Generated foliage is persistent. Add log-axis models and vanilla log/leaf tags. Existing 0.7.0 terrain settings and seeds are unchanged.

## 0.7.2 Solar horizon
A circular procedural Sun with shaded warm photosphere and softly animated corona appears during ascent and in space/lunar skies. The landing exclusion area, world seed, 4096 radius and flight mechanics remain unchanged. Existing generated chunks are not modified; create a new world for uniform terrain, or explore new chunks in a backed-up save. No new registered block types in this update.

## 0.7.5 Rocket item & lunar plains
Titan-1 is now a creative inventory item in the Mega Rockets tab. Right-click the top of a block to deploy the real 14x64 rocket ten blocks farther in the direction you face, on the same level; the center must have solid support and the volume must be empty. In orbit, right-click air to deploy 16 blocks ahead. Works in any dimension on a suitable surface, without replacing blocks. Creative sneak-right-click on an empty idle rocket removes it. Launch mission still starts in the Overworld, and the existing lunar flight/return commands are unchanged. Lunar plains occupy larger areas and have reduced relief, and tree spacing is 12 rather than 16 blocks, with higher placement probabilities. Landing clearance remains protected. Use a new world for consistent terrain; new items also work in existing worlds.

## 0.8.0 Interplanetary flight
Three rocket items: Titan-1 300, Phantom 600, Hyperion 1000 blocks/s at 20 TPS. New variants have separate entity and item IDs, colored hulls, modified hull contours and longer plumes. All use the same 14x64 collision envelope and observation seat. Select Moon or Mars in clickable chat after boarding or /rocket destination moon|mars, then /rocket launch. Default is Moon. Destination and rocket type persist across dimension transfers and saves. Mars is a continuous seeded terrain dimension, not yet a finite physical sphere, with red plains, canyons, craters, caves, 3 custom blocks using vanilla textures, a protected landing platform and 38% gravity. Distances are game-scale 6000 and 24000 blocks, with separate orbit lanes. Flight starts from Earth only; return command remains an emergency teleport. No fuel or survival crafting for rockets. Existing lunar terrain unchanged; back up saves before updating.

## 0.8.2 Finite Mars & lunar masonry
Mars has a finite circular terrain footprint (radius 8192, diameter 16384), with empty space beyond the rim; not a physical sphere. Ten seed-dependent, nonoverlapping mountain centers, 700–1200 blocks across, up to 456 blocks of added elevation. Existing caves and landing coordinates retained. Lunar weather gradients are suppressed and natural snow/ice ticking is disabled in lunar dimensions only; old snow blocks are not automatically deleted. ## 0.8.3 Life support
Survival players get a private server-synchronized oxygen boss bar. Vacuum starts on the Moon, Mars, orbit and at Y>=1000 in the Overworld. All four spacesuit armor pieces are required, including on the rocket's external observation seat. The chestpiece stores 600 seconds of oxygen in its item component; refills by 20 seconds per second in breathable areas. Missing suit pieces or an empty tank cause 4 damage every 10 ticks, bypassing armor. Creative/spectator and adventure modes are exempt. Custom suit icons and armor layers; craft with iron, glass and redstone, available in creative. Survival players can board and launch existing rockets, select destinations and emergency return from vacuum. Rocket items remain creative-only (no survival recipe yet). No terrain changes. Oxygen is not affected by water-breathing potions; ordinary underwater breathing remains separate.

## 0.8.4 Pressurized cockpit & oxygen station
A craftable oxygen station refills the worn spacesuit tank instantly in exchange for one water bucket (empty bucket returned; no power system yet). Right-click the placed block with a water bucket in inventory; creative users need no fuel. All three rockets have a pressurized seated cockpit in their upper stage, genuine translucent panoramic windows, illuminated consoles, pilot chair and life-support rack. Boarding protects from vacuum and recharges equipped oxygen at 20 seconds per second; dismounting restores normal vacuum rules. Press R while seated to open a non-pausing live navigation panel (destination, altitude, speed, progress, launch); commands remain available. Cockpit is a fixed seated entity interior, not a walkable room, and instruments are decorative 3D geometry with actual controls in the R screen. No new terrain generation.

## 0.9.0 Natural landing (Expeditions foundation)
New Moon and Mars terrain has no artificial landing platforms or raised landing aprons. The landing tile remains available to builders. The autopilot surveys nearby natural terrain around the destination origin, using a 16-block search grid up to 512 blocks out, checking a 15x15 footprint for <=1 block of height difference, 3-block-deep natural support, a dismount location, world bounds, collision and entities. One plausible chunk-loading survey every five ticks; if no site is found the ship waits in orbit and repeats the search. Coordinates persist in the rocket's save; descent pauses if blocks obstruct the hull. Existing generated terrain and old builds are not removed. New world recommended for natural terrain; old landed ships retain their old coordinates. Natural landing selection is automatic, not a manual map UI. Further expedition features are roadmap, not implemented.

## 1.0.0-alpha Universe Expansion

Five selectable routes: Moon 12000, Venus 60000, Mars 80000, Europa 220000, Titan 320000 blocks. These are compressed gameplay distances, not an astronomical uniform scale; the computer also shows illustrative Earth-to-body reference distances in km (actual distances vary). 12 original 64px block textures, four procedurally shaded planet disks, new independent cylindrical hull textures and inventory sprites for Titan-1, Phantom and Hyperion. Different hull profiles and exhaust colors. Hyperion acceleration and angular response reduced, interpolation extended to five ticks, flight status refreshed once per second. ## 1.2.0-alpha Free Flight
Celestial mean diameters scaled to the 8192-block Moon; radius rounded to multiples of 16. Mercury 11488, Venus 28544, Earth visual 30048, Mars 15968, Jupiter visual 329632, Saturn visual 274560, Uranus visual 119584, Neptune visual 116096, Europa 7360 and Titan 12128 blocks across. M exits into stationary hold (does not resume canceled route). Earth return still uses /rocket return, not a physical reentry. No orbital station yet.

## 1.4.0-alpha Direct Entry & Manual Landing
Manual piloting now detects nearby surface destinations automatically, independent of the selected route, including Earth (orbit X=-3500, Z=0). Entry zones use existing arrival coordinates X=route distance, Z=lane. Within 2800 blocks, a temporary expiring server chunk ticket loads a 5x5 patch (25 full chunks) and the cockpit displays progress. Within 900 blocks, the rocket waits if loading is incomplete, then transfers intact into the destination world 160 blocks above the highest local terrain, without triggering the automatic descent. The entry region depends on approach position (up to +/-1200 surface blocks relative to the planet origin or Earth's spawn). Release B to enter; B delays transfer. Fly with WASD, Ctrl to descend, Space to rise; manual throttle, terrain clearance-based limits, swept collision and stable support detection aid touchdown. ## 1.5.0-alpha Solar scale data
Mean radii from NASA JPL https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html and https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/ . Moon radius 4096 blocks corresponds to 1737.4 km; all radii rounded to the nearest block. Mars terrain uses the same calculated radius. Reference travel distances for planets are absolute differences of mean solar orbital radii (a same-side circular approximation, not live Earth distances). NASA Planet Compare https://solarsystem.nasa.gov/planet-compare/?compare=planetary&p=1%2C2%2C3%2C4%2C5%2C6%2C7%2C8 . Europa and Titan add their mean satellite orbital radii (JPL https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/). Travel scale 1 block per 2000 km for these routes; Moon stays 12000 blocks for safe flight. Lanes remain gameplay offsets and are not astronomical coordinates. True uniform scale would exceed Minecraft's world border; separate scales preserve playability. Far sky bodies use reference angular size with a visibility floor, close arrival is enlarged. Finish flights before updating; old orbit coordinates no longer match new routes. Back up saves; newly generated edge chunks reflect updated terrain radius.

Loading screens: every landable body and Earth get a destination-themed replacement for vanilla DownloadingTerrainScreen (globe image, palette, facts and animated indeterminate network sync bar). Orbit loads use the current target theme when known. The manual pre-entry HUD displays actual server terrain ticket progress; giant planets display their own themed orbital approach panel, not a fabricated terrain loading percent. No delay is added to dimension switching. Requires client mod as before. Rendering needs an in-game test; vanilla loading fallback retained outside supported dimensions.

## 1.6.0-alpha Living Solar System
NASA fact sheet solar-day lengths, sidereal rotations and approximate orbital periods (https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/) drive persistent world-time clocks. One Earth day is 24000 ticks (20 minutes at 20 TPS); local days retain physical ratios and Venus rotates retrograde. Six landable destinations have unfrozen dimension time, with a shared lunar clock across slices; vanilla Earth time remains. Sun angle, sky/fog brightness and stars vary with day/night. All major planets follow circular coplanar heliocentric paths, moons orbit their parents (enlarged moon separations for gameplay). Epoch starts at world creation, not the current real ephemeris; no eccentricity, tilt, seasonal latitude or N-body integration yet. Positions update every tick and drive entry gates, departure, HUD and autopilot; old fixed travel coordinates are replaced. New Earth launch uses the same moving-target navigator. Equirectangular Earth/Moon NASA maps and nine procedural maps rotate with sidereal periods; illuminated hemispheres use the Sun direction, Saturn has geometric rings. Mars dust haze and Venus/Titan cloud layers drift and fade above the atmosphere. Main asteroid belt generates deterministic solid stone, basalt and iron-bearing rocks at 2.1–3.3 AU, in new space chunks only. Asteroids remain static block structures, not independently orbiting entities; collision sweep stops the rocket, no obstacle avoidance. Atmospheric entry heat is a short-lived visual/telemetry effect based on approximate density and arrival speed; no damage or thermodynamic simulation. NEW WORLD recommended: orbit generator and coordinates changed; existing generated chunks not rewritten, old flights should be finished before upgrade. Full runtime rendering and travel require in-game testing.

## 2.0.0-alpha Lunar settlements and solar energy
Replaces the one fixed colony with multiple deterministic villages generated on suitable terrain in the north lunar slice. Candidate regions are 640 blocks wide, with seed-dependent offsets, biome and slope screening. Each accepted site has six homes, community centre, greenhouse, residents and a pad; `/rocket village` finds nearest predicted site. Existing chunks are not rewritten: a new world is recommended. Face-adjacent connected networks require a battery; every second panels with unobstructed sky generate units based on local solar time, weather and a compressed solar-distance factor. Batteries store up to 60000 units each; oxygen stations consume 1 unit/s even without a nearby player while their network ticks. Night/shadow reduce output; disconnected or exhausted oxygen stations no longer supply rooms. Manual oxygen refills in vacuum require power in survival. All new village homes have roof arrays and batteries; Aurora has independent module arrays and the workshop uses the wing arrays. Existing bases need retrofitting. Right-click panels/batteries to see telemetry. Networks use loaded chunks only, 4096 visited-cell budget, no external energy API, no offline generation. Game energy units are not physical watts. Full runtime playtest still needed.
